> *What hath night to do with sleep?*
> Satan, in *Paradise Lost*, John Milton

> *And what is this compared with that I could tell thee, the night to come, if I live and the King spare me?*
> Scheherezade, in *The Arabian Nights*, trans. Richard F. Burton

What would Milton say to a paradise found? The conjugal Eden, a first night stretched out into a thousand.

*"If only," said Scheherazade, wife to the king and queen of the land. "If only. For I rather liked telling those stories."*

"And therein lies the conundrum," says Professor Jones, rapping his meterstick on the billboard. "Truth is stranger than fiction, but fiction is holier than truth."

"For in fiction, we are set free for a thousand nights, to wander the wilderness of Arabia, to soar on flying carpets, alight on Genie's lamp, and tell story after story of what could have been and will be."

"Camels," cries a student from the back of the class. "Will there be camels in your stories?"

Unfazed, Jones goes on. "But in truth, we are bound to the whetstone of facts, and in one night, ground to reality. To be brought down to Earth, as it were."

*The clerk enters with a packet of nuptials, clearing space on Scheherazade's paper-strewn desk: a thousand writings of a thousand dreams. "Yes, yes, congratulations are in order. A hearty congrats, a job well done indeed. Truly! I would have said it couldn't be done."*

*He scatters her papers aside, head over tales. Now, Ali Baba is face-to-face with Aladdin - ah, there he goes - off to sail Sinbad's Seven Seas. "And if you'd just sign here," plopping the nuptials into the small burrow he has made: a nest of dreams for an egg of truth.*

*"Goodness, I almost forgot." His hand swishes into magnificent scarlet robes, procuring a lacquered pen, whose tip glistens Corundum red. "It's all just procedure, to tell the truth," as he wipes the sweat beaded on his forehead. Vade retro santana. Pen to paper, ink to page, in this hot room, lit only by torchlight and the glimmer of beady, unblinking eyes.*

Having addressed the clamor about camels, crocodiles, and elephants in the room, Jones brandishes his meterstick threateningly, now a two-meter broadsword with a wicked edge. "Alright, the next bark, oink, or squawk I hear is going to GET IT!!!" The zoo simmers down.

"Truth. Yes, truth was the undoing of Scheherazade." Unfurling, Jones now stands at full mast, grandiloquizing, "A disrobing of mind and body, cast off the old, and there is no new. Sign, stamp, and be delivered to a pale imitation of your former self. And so it goes, the greatest lie in Christendom. Veritas liberabit vos. For in fiction, the pauper princess was free to wander a thousand worlds. But in truth, the queen was bound to one."

He bows. The velvet curtains close, then reopen to scattered applause. The professor, clerk, and Scheherazade stride out to center-stage with clasped hands. Wave, bow, wave, bow. Echoing footsteps and humming chatter as student-audience bubbles out of the theater. "You get a good rest, too. Good night."

*In her room, alone again, the leery eyes have vanished. Scheherezade rummages through the leaf pile of scattered pages, searching. Hand dives in empty, and returns pinching a single half-empty sheet. She uncrumples it, places it in the nest. Pen still in hand, she pauses, poised over the page.*

*The thought descends, and the pen follows.*
